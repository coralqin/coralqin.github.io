About

I am a scholar of language assessment and education policy, with a particular focus on how evaluation practices shape access, opportunity, and identity — especially for immigrants and multilingual learners navigating high-stakes systems.

My doctoral dissertation examines the role of language assessment in Canadian immigration processes, centering the lived experiences of Chinese immigrants. I bring together policy analysis, Rasch measurement modeling, structural equation modeling, and computational text analysis to understand these complex systems from multiple angles.

I have taught at the graduate level at the University of Ottawa, contributed to large-scale international editorial projects with Wiley and Routledge, and presented research across North America, Europe, and Asia. Before academia, I worked in banking, luxury hospitality, and — briefly — Walt Disney World.

I am fluent in English and written Chinese, proficient in Cantonese and Mandarin, and endlessly curious about the politics of language.
