01 — About

Coral Yiwei Qin

I am a scholar of language assessment and education policy, examining how evaluation practices shape access, opportunity, and identity for immigrants and multilingual learners navigating high-stakes systems.

My doctoral dissertation reconceptualizes language testing for immigration as a technology of migration governance. Combining policy archaeology with participatory co-analysis involving Chinese immigrants in Canada, I examine how language tests construct the boundaries of the "ideal immigrant" and shape migrants' access to mobility, belonging, and opportunity.

I have taught at the graduate level at the University of Ottawa, where my courses have addressed second language teaching trends and the intersection of technology, languages, and education. I have contributed to large-scale international editorial projects with Wiley and Routledge, and presented research across North America, Europe, and Asia. I remain endlessly curious about the politics of language.

Teaching & Instruction

My teaching is grounded in critical inquiry and a commitment to connecting theory with practice. I create space for students to engage with contested ideas, develop scholarly voices, and think carefully about the social consequences of language and assessment.
